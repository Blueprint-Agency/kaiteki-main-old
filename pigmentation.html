Pigmentation: What is it and How Does it Affect Our Skin?

Pigmentation refers to the coloring of the skin, hair, or eyes. It occurs due to the presence of melanin, a pigment produced by specialized cells called melanocytes. Melanin comes in two main forms: eumelanin, which is brown to black in color, and pheomelanin, which is yellow to red in color. The amount and type of melanin present in the skin determine its color.

Type of Pigmentation

Hyperpigmentation
This occurs when patches of skin become darker in color than the surrounding skin. It can be caused by various factors such as sun exposure, hormonal changes, inflammation, or skin injuries.
Hypopigmentation
This is the opposite of hyperpigmentation, where patches of skin become lighter in color than the surrounding skin. It can be caused by conditions like vitiligo, certain fungal infections, or as a side effect of some treatments.
Albinism
Albinism is a congenital disorder characterized by a lack of melanin pigment in the skin, hair, and eyes. People with albinism typically have very light skin, white or light-colored hair, and light-colored eyes.
Post-inflammatory Hyperpigmentation (PIH)
This type of pigmentation occurs as a result of skin inflammation or injury, such as acne, eczema, or a cut. After the inflammation or injury resolves, dark spots or patches may remain on the skin temporarily.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can Pigmentation be removed permanently?

The simple answer is yes. However, hyperpigmentation treatment does not prevent future discoloration. So, if you continue to damage your skin with sun exposure, acne, or other environmental factors, you may require future treatments.

Can dermatologist cure pigmentation?

Laser peel – The laser peel is a safe and effective way to treat skin pigmentation. The dermatologist uses a laser device to send wavelengths into the targeted areas on your skin. The laser wavelengths destroy the damaged skin cells and reduce the pigmentation.

Why is my pigmentation increasing?

Hyperpigmentation is caused by an increase in melanin. Melanin is the natural pigment that gives our skin, hair and eyes their color. A number of factors can trigger an increase in melanin production, but the main ones are sun exposure, hormonal influences, age and skin injuries or inflammation.
